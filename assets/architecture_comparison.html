Primer What a Dual number is, and why generic cost grows with partials ▶

ForwardDiff replaces a plain number $T$ with a dual number — a value carrying a tangent — built on a nilpotent $\varepsilon$:

$$ \tilde T = T + \varepsilon\,\partial T, \qquad \varepsilon^2 = 0 $$

Every arithmetic primitive is overloaded to propagate the tangent exactly (no truncation error, unlike finite differences). Because $\varepsilon^2$ vanishes, each overload is a differentiation rule in disguise — and composing them yields the chain rule mechanically:

$$ \begin{aligned} (a + \varepsilon a') + (b + \varepsilon b') &= (a+b) + \varepsilon\,(a' + b') \\[2pt] (a + \varepsilon a')\,(b + \varepsilon b') &= ab + \varepsilon\,(a b' + a' b) + \underbrace{\varepsilon^2 a'b'}_{=\,0} \\[2pt] \ln(a + \varepsilon a') &= \ln a + \varepsilon\,\frac{a'}{a} \\[2pt] \Rightarrow\quad f(T + \varepsilon\,\partial T) &= f(T) + \varepsilon\, f'(T)\,\partial T \quad \text{for any composition } f \end{aligned} $$

In a cycle solver the tangent is a vector: the Newton solver seeds $\partial T = \nabla_{\!x} T \in \mathbb{R}^N$, the sensitivity of this temperature to all $N$ solver unknowns ($N \approx 8$–$12$ in a PowerCycles-style residual program). Then $h(\tilde T)$ automatically emerges carrying $\nabla_{\!x} h = c_p(T)\,\nabla_{\!x} T$ — a full Jacobian row block per call.

The cost model behind the scoreboard. The NASA-9 enthalpy evaluation costs roughly $K \approx 25$ scalar primitives (Horner chain, one divide, one $\ln$). Generic Dual arithmetic makes every one of them also do $N$ tangent operations; the analytic rule collapses all of that into two plain-Float64 evaluations and a single $N$-wide scaling:

$$ \text{generic:}\;\; \sim K(1+N)\ \text{ops} \qquad \text{vs} \qquad \text{rule:}\;\; \underbrace{2K}_{h(T),\ c_p(T)\ \text{in Float64}} + \underbrace{N}_{c_p \cdot \partial T} $$

Measured exactly so (scoreboard harness, dh/dT per call): generic Dual grows 11.5 ns → 26.0 ns → 31.4 ns as $N$ goes $1 \to 8 \to 12$; the analytic rule stays flat at 10.9–11.3 ns, because the only $N$-dependent work left is one multiply of the partials vector.

AD rule Analytic Dual rules: dh/dT = cp, ds0/dT = cp/T 11.3 ns @ 12 partials ▶

The ForwardDiff package extension (a weakdep — the base package stays AD-free) replaces Dual arithmetic through the polynomials with closed forms: one Float64 evaluation of the value, one of the derivative, one N-wide multiply. Measured at 12 partials: 11.3 ns vs 31.4 ns generic Dual (2.8×) — and within 1.6× of the plain Float64 call.

$$ h(600) = 304{,}461.195\ \mathrm{J/kg}, \qquad c_p(600) = 1050.9993\ \mathrm{J/(kg\,K)} $$

AD rule Inversions: implicit function theorem, never the loop 131.9 ns @ 8 partials ▶

Differentiating a Newton loop drags Duals through every iteration (measured 282 ns at 8 partials). The implicit function theorem says you never have to: the converged solution defines $T(h_\text{spec})$ implicitly through the residual $F(T, h_\text{spec}) = h(T) - h_\text{spec} = 0$, so differentiating the equation (not the algorithm) gives the exact sensitivity:

$$ \mathrm{d}F = \underbrace{\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}}_{c_p(T)}\,\mathrm{d}T + \underbrace{\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_\text{spec}}}_{-1}\,\mathrm{d}h_\text{spec} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\partial T}{\partial h_\text{spec}} = \frac{1}{c_p(T^\star)} $$

The rule therefore solves the primal in plain Float64 and attaches the partials in one division at the converged $T^\star$:

Measured: 131.9 ns at 8 partials — 2.1× faster than Duals-through-the-loop, only ~1.1× above the underived 121 ns solve.

Worked example — invert dry-air enthalpy back to 1200 K. $$ \frac{\partial T}{\partial h} = \frac{1}{c_p(1200)} = \frac{1}{1174.6222} = 8.5134 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{K/(J/kg)} $$

Sanity-checked three ways with this package: the IFT rule returns 8.5133757872e-4; a central finite difference $\big(T(h\!+\!1) - T(h\!-\!1)\big)/2$ gives 8.5133757864e-4 (agreeing to 9 digits — the residual difference is the FD truncation error, not ours); and the physical reading is sensible: raising the target enthalpy by 1 kJ/kg raises the answer by $\approx 0.85$ K, the reciprocal of the local heat capacity. Note what the loop trace didn't contribute: none of the five iterations carried a tangent. Differentiating the loop would have paid Dual arithmetic in all five — and converged to the same number, because Newton's converged result depends only on the equation, not the path.

The isentrope, with two seeded inputs. T_isentropic defines $T_2$ implicitly through $G(T_2, T_1, \mathrm{PR}) = s_0(T_2) - s_0(T_1) - \tfrac{R}{\eta_p}\ln(\mathrm{PR}) = 0$. Total differential, using $\mathrm{d}s_0/\mathrm{d}T = c_p/T$:

$$ \frac{c_p(T_2)}{T_2}\,\mathrm{d}T_2 \;=\; \frac{c_p(T_1)}{T_1}\,\mathrm{d}T_1 \;+\; \frac{R}{\eta_p\,\mathrm{PR}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{PR} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\partial T_2}{\partial T_1} = \frac{c_p(T_1)\,T_2}{c_p(T_2)\,T_1}, \qquad \frac{\partial T_2}{\partial \mathrm{PR}} = \frac{R\,T_2}{\eta_p\,\mathrm{PR}\,c_p(T_2)} $$

Hand calculation at the scoreboard's compression point ($T_1 = 288.15$ K, $\mathrm{PR} = 12$, $\eta_p = 1$), where the primal solve gives $T_2 = 580.5592$ K, $c_p(T_1) = 1004.278$, $c_p(T_2) = 1046.545$, $R = 287.052$:

$$ \frac{\partial T_2}{\partial T_1} = \frac{1004.278 \cdot 580.5592}{1046.545 \cdot 288.15} = 1.93341, \qquad \frac{\partial T_2}{\partial \mathrm{PR}} = \frac{287.052 \cdot 580.5592}{12 \cdot 1046.545} = 13.2699\ \mathrm{K} $$

ForwardDiff through the extension returns dT2/dPR = 13.2699242259 — matching the hand calculation exactly. (Physical reading: one more unit of pressure ratio buys ≈13.3 K of compressor exit temperature at this point; inlet sensitivity is amplified ≈1.93× across the isentrope.) The three Dual methods at ext L57–L74 implement exactly these formulas for $T_1$ seeded, $\mathrm{PR}$ seeded, or both — in the both-seeded case the two tangent contributions simply add, as the total differential says they must.
